# Offer email playbook

How I write offer-led marketing emails for a whole customer database. It came out of hotel and hospitality work, but the rules hold for most consumer brands.

The short version: **start with the reader's problem, explain why the offer exists, state the terms plainly, and ask for one thing.**

## 1. Write an email, not a landing page

An email is a letter, not a web page.

- A greeting
- Short paragraphs
- One call to action
- A sign-off from a person

Leave out the bold section headers and long descriptive blocks. If it needs a hero banner and six sections, it's a landing page, so link to one.

Write for the whole database unless you've been told to target a segment. Don't gate the offer behind "as a member" if most readers aren't members.

## 2. Open with the problem, not the offer

Don't lead with a feature or a discount percentage. Start with the moment the reader is in: the event that always sells out, the rooms that disappear, the plan they keep putting off. Let them recognise themselves in it, then bring in the offer as the answer.

> **Feature-first:** Enjoy your third night free this winter.
>
> **Problem-first:** Every December, the same thing happens. The Christmas market weekend books up by October, and anyone who leaves it late ends up an hour out of town.

## 3. Make the timeline explicit

If things sell out, say which ones and when. "Book early" is easy to ignore. "The two weekends around the marathon usually go first" is something the reader can act on.

## 4. Explain the why, not just the discount

A discount on its own reads as desperation. Tell the reader why this offer exists and why it's good value. That might be the season ("winter weekends are when a slower trip makes sense"), the product, or the moment.

It's guest education as much as promotion.

## 5. State the terms plainly

Name the offer, the minimum and the end date in plain words.

> **Soft:** Available through to the spring.
>
> **Plain:** Book two nights and the third is free. The offer ends 28th February 2027.

No asterisks hiding the catch.

## 6. Split "what's on" by location, as lists

If you run more than one site, give each its own short bulleted list of events and dates. Under each list, add one line of prose that ties the activity back to the product.

> **Northbank**
> - Christmas market, 20th November to 23rd December
> - Riverside lights trail, every evening from 1st December
>
> Both are a ten-minute walk away, so you can head out after dinner and be back in the warm without booking a taxi.

## 7. One call to action

One button, one verb, one destination. "Book your stay." Not "Book, browse, follow us and refer a friend."

## Checklist

- [ ] Reads like a letter: greeting, short paragraphs, sign-off
- [ ] Opens on the reader's problem or moment, not the offer
- [ ] Says what sells out first, and when
- [ ] Explains why the offer is good value, not just what it is
- [ ] Terms and end date stated plainly
- [ ] What's-on content as lists, split by site, each with a line tying it back
- [ ] One call to action
- [ ] Written for everyone on the list unless it's targeted
